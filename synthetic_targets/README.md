# Synthetic 1/f^β targets with controlled autocorrelation

Generated by `../gen_synthetic_targets.py` (numpy and matplotlib only; about 15 s). All parameters are set at the top of the script.

## Method
- **Field.** In the frequency domain, |F(f)| = f^(-β/2) with f = radial integer frequency in cycles/image and F(0) = 0. Phases are uniform on [0, 2π). The field is the real part of `ifft2`.
- **Size.** 256×256 with 3 channels. Each channel has its own seed `SEED_BASE + 100000·β_idx + 10·img + ch`.
- **β values.** 4.0, 3.5, …, 0.0, with 20 images per β.
- **Normalization (same for every β).** Each channel is z-scored, then multiplied by 1/3 (so ±3σ maps to ±1) and clipped to [-1, 1].
- **Statistics.** All statistics are computed on the saved (clipped) float32 images.
- **Power spectrum.** Radially averaged over 20 images × 3 channels. The slope is a log-log linear fit over 4–64 cycles/image.
- **Autocorrelation.** Each channel is mean-subtracted. The circular autocorrelation is computed via FFT (Wiener–Khinchin) and normalized so that lag 0 = 1. It is radially averaged over rounded distance (circular lag distance) for lags 0–32, then averaged over 20 images × 3 channels.

## Observations
- Fitted slopes are within 0.02 of -β. Clipping affects ≤ 0.27% of pixels (the Gaussian 3σ tail), so the spectrum is effectively unchanged.
- Lag-1 autocorrelation decreases monotonically with β. It is above 0.98 for β ≥ 3, drops quickly between β = 2.5 (0.93) and β = 1.5 (0.49), and is about 0 at β = 0.
- The paper's prior samples (lag-1 ≈ 0.96, lag-32 ≈ 0.27–0.35) fall **between β = 2.5 and β = 3.0**:
  - β = 2.5 has the lowest RMSE. It matches well at lags ≥ 16 but is too low at lags 1–8.
  - β = 3.0 has the best curve shape (Pearson 0.997) but is too high at long lags.
  - So the natural-image priors behave like β ≈ 2.7. That is near the classic β ≈ 2 of natural images, but somewhat steeper, which is plausible for 256² face/bedroom images.
- To test where the weak prior fails, β ≤ 2.0 gives targets whose local correlation is clearly below anything seen in Table 2.

## Medical targets (real chest X-rays)

### Dataset
- **Name:** NLM **Montgomery County Chest X-ray Set**. Images are 4020×4892 PNG (stored 8-bit, from 12-bit acquisitions); the set includes manual lung masks.
- **Source:** <https://data.lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/public/Tuberculosis-Chest-X-ray-Datasets/Montgomery-County-CXR-Set/MontgomerySet/>. No registration is needed.
- **Subset used:** the first 20 files in sorted order, MCUCXR_0001_0 … MCUCXR_0027_0 (all normal cases). About 90 MB with masks, in `../data/montgomery/`.
- **Terms of use** (`../data/montgomery/NLM-MontgomeryCXRSet-ReadMe.pdf`):
  - Research use.
  - **Do not share the data outside your research group.** This also applies to the derived `images_medical/`.
  - Attribute NLM/NIH and cite:
    - Candemir et al., IEEE TMI 33(2):577–590, 2014 (doi:10.1109/TMI.2013.2290491)
    - Jaeger et al., IEEE TMI 33(2):233–245, 2014 (doi:10.1109/TMI.2013.2284099)

### Preprocessing (`load_medical()` in the script)
1. Take the union of the left and right lung masks and find its bounding box.
2. Crop a square centred on that box. Its side is the larger box dimension, rounded down to a multiple of 256 and shifted to stay inside the image. This keeps the thorax and avoids the black border.
3. Downsample to 256×256 with a block mean (area resize, factor ≈ 11–15).
4. Map the pixel range [0, 1] linearly to [-1, 1]; no clipping is needed.
5. Copy the single channel to 3 channels.
6. Save to `images_medical/NNN.{npy,png}`. `images_medical/sources.csv` maps each index to its original file.

### Autocorrelation (non-periodic images)
1. Subtract the mean.
2. Zero-pad to 512×512 and compute |FFT|² → IFFT.
3. Divide by the overlap count (N−|dx|)(N−|dy|).
4. Normalize so that lag 0 = 1.
5. Radially average over rounded integer distance for lags 0–32.

For a fair comparison, `images_gray/beta_X.X/` holds grayscale synthetic twins (channel 0 copied to all 3 channels, same normalization), and their autocorrelation uses this same padded method. These padded grayscale curves differ from the circular RGB curves by ≤ 0.015 at every lag (`autocorr_gray_padded.csv` vs `autocorr.csv`).

- **Equivalent β for the chest X-rays ≈ 3.5.** The CXR curve lies almost on top of β = 3.5 (RMSE 0.007; 0.013 over all lags 0–32).
- The X-ray targets are therefore **smoother** (more locally correlated) than the CelebA/Bedroom prior samples in Table 2 (β ≈ 2.5–3). They sit at the high-correlation end of the synthetic range rather than in the regime where the weak prior might fail.
- This smoothness partly comes from the ≈ 12× area downsampling of a 4k image to 256²; a tighter crop or less downsampling would lower the equivalent β.
